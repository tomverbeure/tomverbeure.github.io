where else?

![TDS7104 in the trunk of my car](/assets/tds7104/tds7104_in_car.jpg)

# Why the TDS7104?

I've been gradually increasing the maximum specifications of my oscilloscopes because
the day will come where I'll need them for sure.

My Tektronix TDS794D is the bandwidth leader with 2 GHz at 4 Gs/s but it needs some serious
amount of tender loving care before it can be deployed in the lab: the gorgeous color CRT is 
broken and must be replaced with an LCD panel, and booting up is hit or miss due to an almost
empty battery backup RAM.  It's a nice scope, but a bit limited in terms of fancy 
measurement features and I/O interface, no Ethernet, and it only accepts 50 Ohm probes.

My TDS684B has a 1 GHz bandwidth at 5 Gs/s, but its cool 
[CCD based acquistion architecture](/2025/05/04/TDS684B-CCD-Memory.html)
comes with trigger and recording length limitations. Its backup battery RAM is also
on its last legs. This scope will be sold eventually.

Earlier this year, I modded a 
[600MHz Agilent 54831 to 1 GHz](/2026/03/28/Repair-of-Two-Agilent-54831-Oscilloscopes.html#upgrade-to-1-ghz-bandwidth)
at 4 Gs/s. This scope has some advanced measurement features such a jitter
analysis but it lacks a touch screen so you're condemned to a mouse if you want to use
anything more than the most basic features.

So except for the 2 GHz BW of the TDS794D, the TDS7104 is my new king of the hill: 
1 GHz bandwidth, a single channel sample rate of 10 Gs/s that drops down to 2.5 Gs/s
when recording 4 channels in parallel. There are advanced signal analysis and compliance
test features if you have the right license key, and in addition to plenty of physical
buttons, it has a resistive touch screen that is not horrible.

Like the 54832, the TDS7104 has a PC motherboard and runs Windows 2000 Pro Embedded
as general operating system.

# Make an Image of the Hard Drive

Whether the machine boot or not, your first step should always first make an image
of the hard drive, a 6 GB IBM Travelstar in my case. 
[Like the 54831](/2026/03/28/Repair-of-Two-Agilent-54831-Oscilloscopes.html#first-suspect-the-ibm-travelstar-hd), 
I thought that I'd have to open the case to access the drive, but no, just push
on the spring-loaded black cover in the back and pull the drive sled out.

![TDS7104 hard drive sled](/assets/tds7104/tds7104_drive_sled.jpg)

Take the drive out of the sled, plug it into a 
[USB-to-IDE adapter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08KT3F998), and extract the data.
On Windows, I use [HDD Raw Copy Tool](https://hddguru.com/software/HDD-Raw-Copy-Tool/).



# Disassembly

I will continue my tradition of documenting the disassembly of test equipment in detail
because *nobody else does it*. Even though the service manual technically describes how
to do it, a few pictures can go a long way to make it easier.

To access the inside of the scope, you need to remove more than 30 screws. On the plus
side, they're all Torx-15 screws and they are all the same length, so you don't need
to worry about keeping track of which screw goes where.

Still, it takes a while and it validated my recent purchase of this
[cordless screwdriver](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07L78Y72J), recommened by Shirar
over at The SignalPath.

**Unbutton the accessory bag**

This took me longer to figure out that I want to admit: you can just unclick the bag
from the chassis, but the buttons can be very tight and if you're not caseful you can
tear the fabric. Use a flat-head screwdriver right next to each button to pop it off.

**Put the scope upright on its back feet**

It's an unusual arrangment, but the easiest way to dismantle the scope is by putting it
on its back feet: you don't need to remove any screw from the back!

**Remove the top panel**

4 screws through the accessory bag buttons fix the top panel to the chassis.

After removing this panel, you could remove the side panels if you can, but I found it
much easier to remove the bottom panel next.

**Remove the bottom panel**

6 screws.

**Remove the handle and side panels**

With the bottom panel gone, the side panels are a breeze to remove, after
unscrewing the handle.

**Remove the front panel**

This is by far the trickiest part of the whole operation, because unlike the TDSxxx series
scopes, it is very easy to break some of the plastic tabs that keep the panel in place.
Luckily, even if you do break them (as I did!), the result is not catastrophic: you should
be able to put it back firmly where it belongs without noticing anything.

The front panel consists of multiple click tabs: 1 on each side and a bunch at the top and
bottom, and 4 slide rails, 2 on each side. If you force the left and right table out too
much, you will break the slide rails.

So the procedure is as follows:

* with a flat-head screwdrive, slightly lift the left and right tabs in the center
* now slightly lift tabs at the top of the front panel
* finally lift the table at the boot of the panel
* gently slide out the panel out, while keeping the panel as much as possible paralle
  with the chassis

# Winning recipe


## Create a Win2K Pro USB Stick

* Download Win2K Pro ISO from the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/win2kproiso)
* Mount the ISO on your Windows PC with File Explorer
* Install and run WinSetupFromUSB to install the Win2K Pro ISO onto a bootable USB stick
* Select the USB stick as targer
* Auto format with FBinst, use FAT32 file system
* Add to USB disk: Windows 2000/XP/2003 Setup
* Select the mounted Win2K Pro ISO drive as source 
* Do "GO" to copy Win2K Pro onto the USB stick

## Create a Plop boot manager floppy for the TDS7104 to boot from

* RadiSys SF810 motherboard doesn't support booting from a USB stick
* My CDROM doesn't work
* [Download Plop](https://www.plop.at/en/bootmanager/download.html)
* Unzip the file. Look for the 1.44 MB plop.img file
* Use Rawrite32 (Win10 and later only) or Rawrite for Windows (win32 exe) to copy the img file to a floppy

## Win2K Pro First Stage installation

* Insert USB stick with Win2K Pro ISO in the lonely scope USB port
* Do NOT use a USB hub: Plop doesn't support that!
* Insert Plop floppy
* Insert new SSD in the scope
* Set BIOS menu so that the scope boots first from floppy
* Boot from floppy
* Select booting from USB
* Select phase 1 Win2K installation from the menu
* Follow instructions...
* Format SSD drive
* Remove floppy disk from drive
* Reboot

## Win2K Pro Second Stage installation

* Should be booting from the SSD now
* It will take a while but eventually, the setup will require the Win2K USB stick.
  If you don't have a PS/2 mouse, use a hub to connect both mouse and USB stick. It should work.
* Follow instructions.
* Eventually a reboot will happen and Windows will work

You now need to install Tektronix specific drivers...
